May this mind of mine be filled with auspicious resolve.

A walkthrough of the public site, from July 2026 through 27 September 2026. Each item names the page a visitor sees, and the link opens that page on shivasankalpa.org.

The pages added in September tell one story: the Mahā Rudra Puraścaraṇa was completed in May, and the sevā it was offered for — the Veda Gurukulas — is now written down, Gurukula by Gurukula. Beside that record, the home page shows a vidyārthī’s life, and a Knowledge Map lets a reader walk a śākhā from saṃhitā to upaniṣad.

1 · Events

The Mahā Rudra page now records what was completed

https://shivasankalpa.org/events/maharudra

The page opens with the completion. By the anugraha of His Holiness Jagadguru Sri Sri Bharati Tirtha Mahasannidhanam and His Holiness Jagadguru Sri Sri Vidhushekhara Bharati Sannidhanam, the Mahārudra yāga of 16 and 17 May 2026 was completed at Hosakerehalli. Students from several Veda Gurukulas offered the japa; the ācāryas led the homa and the other vaidika rites. The three days had opened on the evening of 15 May.

The next paragraph is the bridge to the rest of the site: in the weeks that followed, the Vṛnda visited more than ten Gurukulas, sat with the ācāryas, and began offering groceries, financial support, and what the students need.

What to pause on

  • What was performed. Friday 15 May through Sunday 17 May, with the rites and the hours, kept as a record.
  • Sevas offered. Pooja sankalpa, Rudrābhiṣeka, alaṅkāra, homa, kalaśa, and anna santarpaṇa, listed by name. This page no longer carries a donate button or a volunteer button. It points onward.
  • Nivedana to the Jagadguru. The letter submitted after the Puraścaraṇa, set out on the page as a patra. The middle of the letter is worth reading aloud: the name Śivasankalpa Vṛnda, given during the vijaya yātrā in Tumakuru; the visits to more than ten Gurukulas; and the line that the Vṛnda serves as a small bridge between society and the Gurukulas.
  • Photographs from 15–17 May, at the foot of the page.
  • Two links at the opening and the close: Activities and Gurukulas.

The Events list says the same thing in one sentence: the Puraścaraṇa of May 2026 is recorded here, it was held for loka kalyāṇa and the Veda Gurukulas, and that sevā continues.

2 · Activities

The sevā is written down, visit by visit

https://shivasankalpa.org/activities

Activities is a new item in the menu. Major festivals stay under Events. Everything else the trust does — Gurukula visits, camps, cultural programmes, and trust notes — is recorded here. A reader can filter by Visits, Trust, Programmes, or Camps. A longer note has its own page: who represented the trust, what was done, what was offered, and a note of what was learned. A short note can remain a card.

Eight notes are on the site. Read oldest first and they tell the story after the Puraścaraṇa. If the meeting is short, open the Sirsi journey and the August note; the others are one click from the list.

4 · Home

From Śiṣya to Guru

https://shivasankalpa.org/#vd-title

On the home page, after “Why Gurukulas”, an illustrated story follows a Veda vidyārthī. Two stories share one player. Press Play, or step with the arrows. The sky moves with the hour. The same pictures speak in Kannada when the site language is Kannada.

  3. Years of listening and repeating
  4. Pada, Krama, Jaṭā, Ghana
  5. Veda and the modern classroom

  1. Rising in Brāhma muhūrta
  2. The sacred bath
  3. Sandhyāvandanam at dawn
  4. Learning the Veda from the Guru
  5. Serving the Guru and the āśrama
  6. The midday meal
  7. Saṃskṛta, mathematics, and science
  8. Play and exercise

Scenes carry a short narration and, where it belongs, a verse with its meaning. The Journey says the years are indicative: study varies with the Veda, the śākhā, and the level, such as Kramānta or Ghanānta. The Day says the hours vary from Gurukula to Gurukula.

5 · Knowledge Map

The Veda, read one śākhā at a time

https://shivasankalpa.org/resources

Knowledge Map is new in the menu. On the home page, under the Veda Vṛkṣa, a button reads “Explore the Vedic Knowledge Map”.

https://shivasankalpa.org/

The page’s own instruction is the walkthrough:

  1. Choose a Veda. Four doors. The Yajurveda opens into its Śukla and Kṛṣṇa halves.
  2. Choose a śākhā. Each śākhā is one living line of recitation, with its own books.
  3. Walk its books. Saṃhitā, then brāhmaṇa, āraṇyaka, and upaniṣad, then the limbs that belong to that śākhā. Open a book for what it is, and for where to hear it or read it.

A commentary sits beside the book it explains. A book shared by two śākhās is written once; the other path links to it. Where an open text could not be checked, the page names the book and says why it is not linked, rather than offering an address that has not been opened.

Open this one in the meeting

Taittirīya — Kṛṣṇa Yajurveda. The page describes it as the śākhā most widely recited in South India, and the one several of our Gurukulas teach. The path runs Saṃhitā, Brāhmaṇa, Āraṇyaka, Taittirīya Upaniṣad, Śvetāśvatara, then the Prātiśākhya and the Āpastamba and Baudhāyana sūtras that can be linked.

Inside a single book, the page offers hear, read, e-text, or commentary, each pointing outward:

https://shivasankalpa.org/resources/taittiriya/taittiriya-samhita

For the Ṛgveda pāṭha, the matching door is Śākala.

The four doors

  • Ṛgveda — Śākala; Śāṅkhāyana (Kauṣītaki)
  • Yajurveda — Śukla: Mādhyandina, Kāṇva. Kṛṣṇa: Taittirīya, Maitrāyaṇī, Kaṭha
  • Sāmaveda — Kauthuma, Rāṇāyanīya, Jaiminīya (Talavakāra)
  • Atharvaveda — Śaunaka, Paippalāda

Śikṣā and Kalpa live on each śākhā’s own path. Vyākaraṇa, Nirukta, Chandas, and Jyotiṣa serve every Veda and sit on a shared shelf: the six Vedāṅgas.

Every outward link was opened and checked before it was placed. The sources are the Vedic Heritage Portal of the IGNCA, GRETIL at the University of Göttingen, Sanskrit Documents, and the Internet Archive. The page points to a few good sources for each book. This edition covers the Veda and its Upaniṣads. Darśana, Itihāsa, and Purāṇa are the shelves still to come.
